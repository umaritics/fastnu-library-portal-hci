Part 2 | Team convergence 
2.1 How the critique ran 
We met on campus on Tuesday 20 October 2026 for about 90 minutes, all four of us, with the Ideate lead facilitating. All 20 
sheets went up on one wall and we worked through them as follows. 
• Silent read. Everyone read all 20 sheets before anyone said anything. 
• Critique of every alternative. For each sheet in turn we agreed one strength, one concern and the requirement it best 
satisfies, each tied to D2 evidence or a D2 success criterion. The author spoke last, so the discussion didn't turn into people 
defending their own ideas. 
• Coverage scoring. We rated each alternative against the R1 to R5 success criteria and noted whether we could realistically 
prototype and test it in D4 and D5. 
• Shortlist and comparison. Anything with at least one Full rating and a testable form went forward. The ones aimed at our 
leading requirement, R2, were compared head to head. 
• Decision. By majority vote. Because the front-runner (MY-1) was the facilitator's own concept, Shahmeer (Test lead) chaired 
the vote instead, and Mirza voted last. 
Grouping by approach. Before the critique we sorted the 20 sheets by what they change. We did this mainly to check that we 
had actually diverged, rather than written 20 versions of the same screen.

Part 4 | Design rationale 
4.1 Concept summary: Free Rooms First 
Room booking is one tap from the portal home page and opens straight onto the day's rooms. Every free start time is a labelled 
button, so a student books by picking a time that is already shown as free, then a length from three fixed buttons. Before 
booking, one sentence states exactly what will be booked. Afterwards, a pass says the room is held and gives a booking ID; the 
pass stays under "My bookings" and can be shared with the group. 
A note on naming: the current portal already has a menu entry called "My Room Bookings" (Appendix A, Figure A1).  
In our design we shorten it to "My bookings", and that is the label used in the storyboard and in the rest of this report. We 
haven't been able to see what that page currently contains, so the pass and booking ID are new.

4.4 Why we selected it 
MY-1 became the core because it is the only one of the 20 alternatives that could meet two D2 success criteria on its own, R2 
and R4, and R2 carries the leading opportunity and POV1. It answers "which rooms are free?" on screen at journey stage 3, the 
step where students most visibly give up on the portal and ask people (P09-B2, P04-E1). And it does this in a single-column form 
that can pass R5 at phone width. SA-1 meets R2 just as well but fails R5 checkpoint 1 at 320 px; MU-2 meets R4 but can't pass 
R2's criterion. 
We added MU-1 and MY-4 because nothing that changes the booking page touches R1 or R3, and each was the only Full rating in 
its column. MU-4's fixed length buttons went in because they remove the slider from the one choice MY-1 still left open. None of 
these changes MY-1's flow, so the result is still one direction rather than a bundle of features, and it stays inside the portal 
instead of adding a desk, kiosk or messaging service. 
The need to know which rooms are free shows up at all three points on the persona spectrum, so the core concept serves
